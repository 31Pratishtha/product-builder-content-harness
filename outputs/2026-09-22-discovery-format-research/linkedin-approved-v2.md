We're planning a small product discovery exercise: take the notes from one user conversation and work out what we still need to ask before deciding what to build.

Suppose we're reviewing those notes and looking for the other ways the person tried to solve their problem. We can't find anything about alternatives. Did they try something else? Did we ask?

At that point, we'd want to leave ourselves a follow-up question. Writing "they had no other option" would give us an answer the conversation never provided.

That's the kind of gap we want to catch with a simple worksheet. We're calling it a decision map, with five questions:

- What made the problem matter at that moment?
- What was the person trying to change?
- What else did they consider or try?
- What made those options difficult or risky?
- What did they do next?

We'd keep the supporting conversation note beside each answer. Anything the notes don't cover stays marked "unknown," ready for a follow-up.

We haven't tested the map yet. For the first attempt, we'll write a plain summary of one conversation and choose our next question. Then we'll fill in the map and see whether we'd ask something different, and why.

Reading the notes twice could explain any change, so we'll record that too. We also want to see whether the worksheet adds enough to justify the extra effort.

If you're an engineer or designer learning discovery alongside us, the question we're starting with is quite small: after reviewing a conversation, can we explain what we need to ask next?
