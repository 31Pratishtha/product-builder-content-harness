## Keep unknowns visible
Reader: emerging builders. Promise: separate account, inference, and missing evidence.
Support: actual brainstorm and planned-method decision; checked interview guidance; implemented worksheet.
Why now: converts the current question into an inspectable artifact.
Risk: extra structure could create false completeness.
Evidence score 1 reflects a real planning decision and artifact, not a tested discovery result.

## Interest and action
Reader: teams with encouraging reactions. Promise: identify a next-action question.
Support: brainstorm hypothesis only. Why now: relevant to early validation.
Gap: no reported interest, requested action, or observed abandonment. Research on formats cannot supply that experience.

## Workaround
Reader: engineers/designers selecting scope. Promise: inspect the steps before automating.
Support: brainstorm hypothesis only. Why now: relevant to workflow tools.
Gap: no observed workaround or resulting product decision.

## Recommendation
Use the first angle. Its specific contribution is how to preserve uncertainty in the proposed worksheet. The LinkedIn post gives one concrete gap example, X breaks the process into steps, and Substack explains the reasoning and test limitations. The research supports editorial choices and interview practice, not claims that our map is validated.
