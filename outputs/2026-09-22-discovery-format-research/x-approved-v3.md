### Post 1

As we learn more about product discovery, we're thinking about the conversations we'll have before choosing what to build. We want to understand how someone deals with a problem today, including the parts we might forget to ask about.

### Post 2

We've put together a simple worksheet to help us go back through those conversations. The idea is to keep the details from the notes alongside anything we still need to understand. We haven't tried it yet, but this is how we're thinking of using it.

### Post 3

We'd start with what was happening when the problem came up and what the person wanted to change. That gives us some context before we look at the options they considered and what they did next.

### Post 4

For example, if our notes don't mention anything else they tried, we'd leave that question open for a follow-up. We'd want to find out whether they tried another tool, found a workaround, or chose to leave things as they were.

### Post 5

Then we'd look at what made an option difficult and how things unfolded. If someone waited before taking action, we'd want to hear more about that time before deciding what held them back.

### Post 6

Together, those questions make up the five parts of the map: trigger, desired progress, alternatives, friction, and action. We'd keep the note behind each answer, mark any guesses, and leave room for details that don't fit.

### Post 7

To try it, we'll summarize one conversation, choose a follow-up question, then fill in the map and see if our question changes. Reading the notes again might explain the difference. We'll also record whether the extra work seems worth it.
