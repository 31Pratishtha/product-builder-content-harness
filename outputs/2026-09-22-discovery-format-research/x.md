## Recommended thread: X-thread

### Post 1

We're exploring a decision map for product discovery: trigger, desired progress, alternatives, friction, action. The rule we want to test: every explanation needs a note behind it, and every gap stays visible. The method is untested.

### Post 2

1. Trigger + progress: ask about one recent occurrence. What made it matter then? What was the person trying to change? Start with their account before deciding which box it belongs in.

### Post 3

2. Alternatives: what did they consider or try? Include doing nothing if that is what they describe. An empty field means we need another question; it does not mean there were no alternatives.

### Post 4

3. Friction + action: what felt difficult, and what happened next? Keep those answers separate. If someone delayed, we still need to ask why. Delay alone does not tell us whether the obstacle was effort, risk, or something else.

### Post 5

4. Beside each entry, keep the supporting note. Put our interpretation in a separate line. A participant's account is not a directly observed event; neither is proof that the same pattern holds for other people.

### Post 6

5. Our first test: compare a plain summary with the map from one conversation. Record any change to the next product question. A second pass might explain the difference. If the map adds only paperwork, we should change it or drop it.
