# Voice calibration: context and natural flow

Source: the human review in outputs/2026-09-22-discovery-format-research/human-review.md. Approved preference, not a statistically validated quality predictor. Use these cases with the voice rubric; do not imitate a topic-specific sentence mechanically.

| Case | Source passage | Expected judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context-free maxim | linkedin.md, LI-primary opening: "A discovery worksheet can look complete even when our understanding is not." | Fail: reader lacks a concrete activity and the opening sounds like a maxim. |
| Guru-like contrast | longform.md opening: "A completed discovery worksheet is not the same thing as a complete explanation." | Fail: quote-like generalization before context. |
| Unnecessary instruction | linkedin.md: "Our interpretation belongs on a separate line." | Fail directness in this paragraph: layout detail adds no useful explanation. Preserve the evidence/inference distinction where needed. |
| Abrupt hypothetical | x-v2.md, primary post 1: the sequence beginning "Suppose we can't find what else the person tried. Did we ask?" | Fail: compresses context, questions, instruction, and caveat into one opening. |
| Connected explanation | x-v3.md, primary posts 1-2 | Human-approved reference: first establish prospective discovery conversations and their purpose, then introduce the worksheet and its untested status. |
| Concise or reference content | Any new checklist or short post | Do not fail merely for lacking a story. Require sufficient context and useful progression for that format. |
| Invented story | Any unsupported personal event, dialogue, or result | Fail evidence integrity even if the story sounds natural. |

All draft paths above are relative to outputs/2026-09-22-discovery-format-research/. For new work, apply the criteria to its complete opening and body, including alternate hooks. Quote any failed sentence. This reference does not grant approval for new content.
